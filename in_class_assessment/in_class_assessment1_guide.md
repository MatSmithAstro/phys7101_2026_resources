# PHYS7101 · In-class assessment 1: what to expect

*Week 6 lab, Monday 9 November 2026 · 5% of the module · a 5–8 minute conversation about your own work*

# What it is

A short conversation with a member of staff (Aneta or one of the PGTAs) about the work you are doing at the moment. In Week 6 that will almost certainly be **Exercise 2**: by then you should have done at least Tasks 1–3. If you have barely started Exercise 2, you can talk about a lab notebook you have worked through (Week 4 or Week 5) instead.

It is not a test of whether your answers are right, and you won't be asked to write code. We want to hear **what you did, why you chose to do it that way, and what you would do next**.

# What to bring

- Your **Exercise 2 notebook, open and already run**, so the outputs are visible: on your own laptop, or on the lab computer you're working at.
- Your **logbook**, if you keep a separate one. If your notes are in the notebook itself (the *Working log* cell at the top of the Exercise 2 notebook, or Markdown cells between your code), that's fine: we will go through the notebook with you.

# What we will ask about

The conversation covers three areas, which are the marking criteria:

| Area | For example |
| --- | --- |
| **Your analysis choices** (including your code and figures) | "Talk me through this plot: what is it telling you?" · "Why did you choose that rule, or that value? What else did you try?" · "Show me where in your code that happens." |
| **Your understanding of the method** | "What does this function actually do?" · "What does this method assume? When would it give the wrong answer?" · "How do you know this result is right?" |
| **Where you go next** | "What are you least sure about, and how would you check it?" · "What would you do next, and why?" |

We will pick the questions from what is in your notebook, so every conversation is different and there is nothing to learn by heart.

# How to prepare (about 20 minutes)

1. Make sure your notebook runs up to where you have got to.
2. Add a few dated notes in the *Working log* (or your logbook): what you tried, what you expected, what happened, what you decided and why, **and what didn't work**. It's fine to write these up now for work you have already done.
3. Pick **one thing you're pleased with** and **one thing you're stuck on or unsure about**. You may be asked about either.

**Being stuck is fine.** A clear explanation of what you tried, why it didn't work and what you would try next is worth as much as a finished task.

# How it is marked

Each of the three areas is placed in a band on the University scale, using the same idea as the exercise grids, and the conversation gets one overall grade: the band where most areas sit, then −, flat or +. You will get the grade and a one-line comment. This is the grid we mark with:

| Band | Your analysis choices (incl. code and figures) | Your understanding of the method | Where you go next |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A+** | As A, and goes beyond the tasks: notices a problem in the data without being asked, and has measured it | As A, and can say what the result would look like if the method's assumptions failed | As A, and the next step would change a conclusion |
| **A− A** | Explains each choice, the alternatives and the evidence, using your own output; finds anything in your code quickly | Explains how the method works, what it assumes and when it fails; has checked it (a known answer, a plot, a second method) | A specific next step that follows from your results; says what you don't yet trust and how you'd test it |
| **B− B B+** | Explains what you did and mostly why; some choices left at defaults without comment | Explains how it works; assumptions or limits only when prompted | A sensible next step, with general reasoning |
| **C− C C+** | Describes what you did, not why; finds things in your code with difficulty | Knows what a function returns, not how or why | Next step is only "finish the tasks" |
| **D− D D+** | Can't explain your choices or find them in your code | Can't explain the method behind your code | None |
| **F** | No work of your own to discuss | | |

At this level D is a fail. If you have kept going with Exercise 2 and can explain your own work, you should expect B or above.

# Working with friends

Talking about the exercise with others is encouraged; the work you submit, and what you explain in this conversation, must be your own. If you worked closely with someone, that's fine: we'll ask *you* to explain *your* choices. The same applies to GenAI: you must be able to explain every line you use.

Conversations happen one at a time. Please keep working at your seat until it's your turn, rather than waiting nearby, and if a conversation is happening at the next computer, carry on with your own work.
